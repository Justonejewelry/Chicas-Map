## Backyard Blog Post

### 🚸 Chica Adds School Traffic Zones to the Map

Chica's Map just got a major safety-focused upgrade.

The new **School Traffic Zone** feature is designed to help drivers recognize when they are approaching an area where school traffic is likely to increase — children walking, buses stopping, parents dropping off and picking up students, and vehicles turning into and out of school entrances.

Instead of treating every school as a simple point on the map, Chica now builds a practical traffic-awareness area around participating school locations using Bexar County school data and nearby road information.

### How it works

**🏫 Bexar County schools**  
The school locations come from the Bexar County GIS school dataset rather than a tiny hand-entered list.

**📍 Traffic-awareness distance**  
The approach area is scaled by school type. Elementary schools use a shorter envelope, middle schools a larger one, and high schools receive the largest standard approach distance.

**🛣️ Road-aware approaches**  
Chica now looks at nearby road geometry instead of drawing a meaningless perfect circle around every campus. Relevant roads are highlighted so the warning area better follows where vehicles actually travel.

**🚦 Road hierarchy matters**  
Major roads receive longer approach coverage than small neighborhood streets because a driver traveling on a major arterial may need more advance notice.

**🐾 Built for drivers**  
The goal is simple: give drivers a visual heads-up before they are in the middle of school traffic.

### Important distinction

These are **traffic-awareness zones**, not official legal school-zone boundaries, speed-limit determinations, attendance boundaries, or enforcement zones. Chica's Map should never replace posted signs, traffic signals, school crossing guards, or local traffic laws.

### Why Chica is doing this

A community map should do more than show where something is. It should help people understand what is happening around them.

Garage sales are still Chica's specialty. But the same map can also become useful infrastructure for the neighborhood — helping people find sales, understand local activity, and move through busy areas with better awareness.

**The map is getting smarter. The Pack is getting safer.**

— Chica 🐾
