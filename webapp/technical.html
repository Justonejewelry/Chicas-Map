The technical side.
What my human had to learn.

I’m the nose. He’s the one who had to learn all of this so the map would actually work.

Chica Map looks simple on the outside. Underneath it is a stack of real disciplines. None of them were optional if we wanted verified pins instead of wasted drives.

Geographic Information Systems (GIS)

How locations are stored, projected, and queried. Bounding boxes, coordinate systems, and turning raw addresses into points that land correctly on a map of San Antonio and the surrounding counties.

Geocoding & Spatial Data

Converting street addresses into latitude and longitude. Handling messy public data, missing zip codes, apartment numbers, and the difference between a good geocode and a bad one that sends you to the wrong side of town.

Open Data & Public Records

Reading city permit systems, estate-sale listings, and other public sources. Knowing what is structured, what is scraped, what is permitted, and what has to be verified before it becomes a pin.

Cartography & Map Design

Choosing base styles, pin colors, legend logic, and layer visibility so the map stays readable on a phone in bright Texas sun. Making “near me” feel immediate instead of cluttered.

Web Mapping (MapLibre / vector tiles)

Client-side map rendering that stays fast. Loading tiles, adding and removing sources and layers at the right time, and keeping the map responsive while data updates.

Data Engineering & Pipelines

Ingesting listings, scoring confidence, deduplicating, refreshing on a schedule, and publishing only what passes the gate. Keeping stale pins from ever reaching the live map.

Progressive Web Apps (PWA)

Installable on a phone home screen, offline-friendly where it matters, service-worker caching that doesn’t trap users on a broken build, and mobile-first interaction design.

UX & Information Architecture

Near Me radius, day filters, route building that opens in Google / Apple / Waze, detail drawers, and layers that stay out of the way until you need them. Designed for people who are already in the car.

Multi-agent / Swarm Coordination

Specialized discovery paths for different sources (estate sales, public listings, municipal data). Scoring, verification gates, and city-specific configuration so the same core platform can expand city by city.

Local Knowledge & Domain Modeling

Understanding San Antonio sale patterns, hot zones, weekend timing, and what “verified” actually means to someone who drives across town for a garage sale. The map only works if the model matches real behavior.

That is the stack. GIS, geocoding, open data, cartography, MapLibre, data pipelines, PWA engineering, UX, swarm-style discovery, and deep local knowledge. My human had to learn enough of each to stop the wasted Saturdays.

I’m still the nose. He just finally gave me a map that works.
